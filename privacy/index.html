FlightLine Privacy Policy

Last updated November 21, 2026

FlightLine collects only what it needs to identify the aircraft you photograph, log your sightings and run your account, and it never sells your data or shows ads.

FlightLine: Plane Spotting (“FlightLine”, “we”) is a plane-spotting app and community published by Joseph Bultena. This policy covers the FlightLine iOS app, including beta versions distributed through TestFlight, and the beta waitlist on getflightline.app. Questions go to support@getflightline.app.

Live aircraft on the Radar

  • Where it comes from. Positions come from ADSB.lol’s open data, which collects what aircraft broadcast publicly. Your iPhone only ever asks our server, which asks ADSB.lol for the airport’s area and filters the result before anything reaches the app. Nothing about you is sent.
  • Never shown. Aircraft whose owners have asked not to be tracked (the FAA’s LADD and PIA programs) are removed on our server and never reach your iPhone. Airport ground vehicles (such as operations, fire and tow vehicles) aren’t aircraft and are removed too.
  • Everything else, in categories you can filter. Airliners, cargo planes, small planes, helicopters, business jets, military and government aircraft (including VIP and executive transports), seaplanes and aircraft we can’t identify all appear on the Radar. You choose what to see with the Radar’s filters. Military and government aircraft appear only when they broadcast their position openly.
  • View-only. Small private planes, helicopters (including medical and police helicopters), business and corporate jets, military and government VIP aircraft, private floatplanes and aircraft we can’t identify show only their type, altitude and speed. They can’t be followed, can’t send alerts or Live Activities, and are never logged as sightings or looked up by registration. We keep no history of them; only commercial airline and seaplane flights count toward a livery’s “last seen”.
  • Matching photos added later. For 30 days we keep a log of the airline and cargo tails our server saw near our airports (registration or transponder code, airport and time), then delete it. When you add a photo without a location and pick the airport, FlightLine asks our server only whether that one tail was seen there around the photo’s time. The photo’s date and location are read on your iPhone; location data is removed from every photo before it’s uploaded or shared. Private, business, helicopter, military and LADD/PIA aircraft are never logged.
  • Likely arrivals and departures. When a flight’s route isn’t known, FlightLine guesses whether it’s arriving or departing from its recent positions near the runways. That guess is made on your iPhone, kept in memory for under an hour and never sent anywhere.
  • Your filters. Your Radar filter choices are saved on your iPhone. If you sign in, they’re also stored with your FlightLine user ID so they follow you to a new iPhone. Only you can read them.
  • No owner details. To fill in aircraft types we use the FAA’s public aircraft registry, storing only each U.S. registration number and its aircraft type. We never read, store or show owner names or addresses.
  • Your own photos. You can still make a card from your own photo of any aircraft, including small planes, helicopters, military aircraft and seaplanes. For seaplane cards, FlightLine checks on your iPhone whether the photo shows water (for a badge); that check doesn’t send the photo anywhere.

Photo safety checks

  • On your iPhone first. Before a photo is uploaded or sent for identification, FlightLine checks it on your device with Apple’s Sensitive Content Analysis. This check runs entirely on your iPhone; Apple and FlightLine don’t receive the photo or the result. iOS makes it available when Sensitive Content Warning or Communication Safety is turned on in Settings.
  • Then during identification. The same request that identifies the aircraft also checks that the photo shows an aircraft and is appropriate for an all-ages app.
  • Rejected photos. A photo that fails either check is discarded: it isn’t saved in FlightLine or uploaded anywhere else. We record only the reason (for example “no aircraft found”), whether it came from the camera or your library, and when, linked to your FlightLine user ID so we can spot misuse. That record never includes the photo.
  • Faces are blurred. If people appear in a photo, their faces are blurred on your card, on anything you share, and in the copy sent for identification. The unblurred original stays on your iPhone.
  • When a check can’t run. If you’re offline, the card is still made on your iPhone but marked unchecked, and it earns no points and can’t be shared until the check runs.

Other things we record

  • Identification usage. For each identification we log which AI model answered, how long it took and how many tokens it used, linked to your FlightLine user ID, to manage cost and limits. Never the photo or the text read from it.
  • Free identifications. If you aren’t signed in, we count how many photos have been identified for your device’s anonymous user (by a random photo ID, not the photo), so we can offer two free and then ask you to sign in.
  • Livery names you suggest. If you name a special livery we don’t know yet, we receive the name, the airline, aircraft type and registration on your card, and a short description of the paint scheme, for review. Not the photo.
  • Tester status. If you sign in during our test period, we record whether you joined in the alpha or beta wave so we can honor any free Pro time we offer testers.
  • Usage events. We record a few named events: opening the app, making a card, starting or ending a spotting session, following a livery and sharing. Each has only your FlightLine user ID, the time and the app version. They never include your location, photos, card details, device model or advertising identifier, and we use no third-party analytics or tracking tools. Only FlightLine’s developer can read them.
  • Leaderboard points. If you sign in, the points each card earns (with the airport and time it was scored) and how many cards and sightings you have are uploaded so you can appear on leaderboards. If you aren’t signed in, they stay on your iPhone and you don’t appear on leaderboards.
  • Badges. Badges and Spotter Passport stamps are worked out on your iPhone from your cards and sessions. If you sign in, we store which badges you’ve earned, when, and which three you chose to feature, linked to your FlightLine user ID. They appear on your Spotter Card unless you make it Private.
  • Spotter Card. If you sign in, you get a public Spotter Card that other signed-in spotters can open from the leaderboard. It shows only your spotter name, home airport, Spotter number, rank, points, card count, featured badges and badge collection, session count, longest session and total spotting time, number of airports visited, rarest catch, favorite airline, the month you joined, a Founding Spotter stamp if you have one, and a hero photo you choose from your own cards. Faces in that photo are blurred and its location and other details are removed before it’s uploaded. It never shows your real name, email, exact spot locations, or session dates and times. It can also show how many of your cards are verified, your Spotter Passport stamps, and up to three rarest catches, each with a small thumbnail of your own photo; faces in thumbnails are blurred and their location and other details are removed before upload. Other spotters can tap any leaderboard row: a Private card shows only the spotter name, position and points. You can make your card Private in Profile: then only you can see it, and its hero photo and thumbnails are deleted from our server.
  • Session time. While a spotting session is running and FlightLine is open, it checks every couple of minutes whether you’re within the airport area, so session-length badges count only time spent spotting. Only “at the airport or not” is kept, on your iPhone; your location is never stored or sent.
  • Spotting locations. While FlightLine is open and you’ve allowed location access, it checks whether you’re at a known spotting location to offer starting a session. That check happens on your iPhone; your location isn’t sent to us for it.

Beta waitlist

  • What we keep. When you join the waitlist on getflightline.app, we store your email address and, if you give them, your home airport and what you like to spot, with the date you joined. We also keep where your request stands (pending, approved or waitlisted) and when that changed, and when we emailed you about it.
  • Reviewing requests. The beta is invite-only, so each request is emailed to FlightLine’s developer to review, with the details above. When we approve you, we email you the TestFlight link to install the beta; if a wave is full, we email you once to say you’re still on the list. Each of these emails is sent once. Installing through TestFlight is covered by Apple’s own privacy policy.
  • Why. Only to send you a beta invite and, rarely, news about the beta. No marketing lists, and we never sell or share it.
  • Confirmation email. When you join, we send one email confirming you’re on the list. It’s sent through Resend, our email provider, which receives only your email address to deliver it. Each address gets this email once, ever: to make sure of that we keep a one-way, salted fingerprint of the address (not the address itself), even if you later leave the list. If someone else entered your address, just ignore the email.
  • Abuse protection. To stop automated sign-ups we keep a one-way, salted hash of your IP address for 24 hours. We never store the address itself.
  • Where. The waitlist is stored with Supabase and can’t be read by anyone but FlightLine’s developer. Resend, our email provider, sends the waitlist emails. The website uses no cookies, analytics or tracking.
  • Removal. Email hello@getflightline.app and we’ll remove you. We delete the waitlist once the beta ends.

Sharing, storage and security

  • No selling, no ads. We don’t sell your data, share it with advertisers or use it for tracking across other apps.
  • What others see. If you sign in, you pick a spotter name. Only that name and your points appear on leaderboards, and the name is printed on cards you share and attached to livery names you suggest. Your real name and email are never shown to other spotters. Cards and photos stay private unless you share them yourself.
  • Reporting a spotter name. If you report a spotter name, we store your FlightLine user ID, the reported spotter and the name, so FlightLine’s developer can review it. The spotter isn’t told who reported them. A name can be sent back for a new pick, and accounts that break the rules can be banned. Spotter names and suggested livery names are checked against a list of offensive terms on our server.
  • Where it lives. Most of your data is stored on your iPhone. Data you sync is stored with Supabase. Photos sent for identification are used to answer that request, not to build a photo library.
  • How long. We keep your account data until you delete your account. Per-device request counts used for rate limiting are kept only as long as needed to enforce daily limits.
  • Security. Data travels over encrypted connections, access to our database is restricted per user, and our service keys are never stored in the app.
  • Legal requests. We disclose data only when required by law.

Your choices

  • Permissions. Camera, photo library and location access are optional and can be changed anytime in iPhone Settings. Without them you can still browse the app, and photos without a location simply earn no points.
  • Delete your account. Go to Account in the app and tap Delete account. This removes your account and the data we store on our servers. Deleting the app removes the data stored on your iPhone.
  • Access or questions. Email support@getflightline.app to ask what we hold about you or to request a copy or correction.
  • Children. FlightLine isn’t directed at children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect their data.
  • Changes. If this policy changes, we’ll update the date at the top and, for significant changes, tell you in the app.
