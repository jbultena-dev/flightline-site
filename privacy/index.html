FlightLine Privacy Policy

Last updated November 17, 2026

FlightLine collects only what it needs to identify the aircraft you photograph, log your sightings and run your account, and it never sells your data or shows ads.

FlightLine: Plane Spotting (“FlightLine”, “we”) is a plane-spotting app and community published by Joseph Bultena. This policy covers the FlightLine iOS app, including beta versions distributed through TestFlight, and the beta waitlist on getflightline.app. Questions go to joe.bultena@gmail.com.

Photo identification happens only when you make a card. Lookups of aircraft photos send no personal information about you.

Photo safety checks

  • On your iPhone first. Before a photo is uploaded or sent for identification, FlightLine checks it on your device with Apple’s Sensitive Content Analysis. This check runs entirely on your iPhone; Apple and FlightLine don’t receive the photo or the result. iOS makes it available when Sensitive Content Warning or Communication Safety is turned on in Settings.
  • Then during identification. The same request that identifies the aircraft also checks that the photo shows an aircraft and is appropriate for an all-ages app.
  • Rejected photos. A photo that fails either check is discarded: it isn’t saved in FlightLine or uploaded anywhere else. We record only the reason (for example “no aircraft found”), whether it came from the camera or your library, and when, linked to your FlightLine user ID so we can spot misuse. That record never includes the photo.
  • Faces are blurred. If people appear in a photo, their faces are blurred on your card, on anything you share, and in the copy sent for identification. The unblurred original stays on your iPhone.
  • When a check can’t run. If you’re offline, the card is still made on your iPhone but marked unchecked, and it earns no points and can’t be shared until the check runs.

Other things we record

  • Identification usage. For each identification we log which AI model answered, how long it took and how many tokens it used, linked to your FlightLine user ID, to manage cost and limits. Never the photo or the text read from it.
  • Free identifications. If you aren’t signed in, we count how many photos have been identified for your device’s anonymous user (by a random photo ID, not the photo), so we can offer two free and then ask you to sign in.
  • Livery names you suggest. If you name a special livery we don’t know yet, we receive the name, the airline, aircraft type and registration on your card, and a short description of the paint scheme, for review. Not the photo.
  • Tester status. If you sign in during our test period, we record whether you joined in the alpha or beta wave so we can honor any free Pro time we offer testers.
  • Usage events. We record a few named events: opening the app, making a card, starting or ending a spotting session, following a livery and sharing. Each has only your FlightLine user ID, the time and the app version. They never include your location, photos, card details, device model or advertising identifier, and we use no third-party analytics or tracking tools. Only FlightLine’s developer can read them.
  • Leaderboard points. If you sign in, the points each card earns (with the airport and time it was scored) and how many cards and sightings you have are uploaded so you can appear on leaderboards. If you aren’t signed in, they stay on your iPhone and you don’t appear on leaderboards.
  • Spotting locations. While FlightLine is open and you’ve allowed location access, it checks whether you’re at a known spotting location to offer starting a session. That check happens on your iPhone; your location isn’t sent to us for it.

Beta waitlist

  • What we keep. When you join the waitlist on getflightline.app, we store your email address and, if you give it, your home airport, with the date you joined.
  • Why. Only to send you a beta invite and, rarely, news about the beta. No marketing lists, and we never sell or share it.
  • Abuse protection. To stop automated sign-ups we keep a one-way, salted hash of your IP address for 24 hours. We never store the address itself.
  • Where. The waitlist is stored with Supabase and can’t be read by anyone but FlightLine’s developer. The website uses no cookies, analytics or tracking.
  • Removal. Email joe.bultena@gmail.com and we’ll remove you. We delete the waitlist once the beta ends.

Sharing, storage and security

  • No selling, no ads. We don’t sell your data, share it with advertisers or use it for tracking across other apps.
  • What others see. If you sign in, you pick a spotter name. Only that name and your points appear on leaderboards, and the name is printed on cards you share and attached to livery names you suggest. Your real name and email are never shown to other spotters. Cards and photos stay private unless you share them yourself.
  • Where it lives. Most of your data is stored on your iPhone. Data you sync is stored with Supabase. Photos sent for identification are used to answer that request, not to build a photo library.
  • How long. We keep your account data until you delete your account. Per-device request counts used for rate limiting are kept only as long as needed to enforce daily limits.
  • Security. Data travels over encrypted connections, access to our database is restricted per user, and our service keys are never stored in the app.
  • Legal requests. We disclose data only when required by law.

Your choices

  • Permissions. Camera, photo library and location access are optional and can be changed anytime in iPhone Settings. Without them you can still browse the app, and photos without a location simply earn no points.
  • Delete your account. Go to Account in the app and tap Delete account. This removes your account and the data we store on our servers. Deleting the app removes the data stored on your iPhone.
  • Access or questions. Email joe.bultena@gmail.com to ask what we hold about you or to request a copy or correction.
  • Children. FlightLine isn’t directed at children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect their data.
  • Changes. If this policy changes, we’ll update the date at the top and, for significant changes, tell you in the app.
